LARA learns transitions between randomly sampled internal layers of a diffusion transformer using a shared predictor conditioned on timestep and source/target depths. The graphical abstract extends the LARA branch of Fig. 1 with a labeled diffusion-transformer backbone driven by a noisy image, highlighted source/target layers, a simple predictor, and examples of random pairs across training iterations. The LARA boundary groups three objectives: prediction, transition consistency, and redundancy. Prediction matches the source-derived state to a stop-gradient target activation using one random pair (a, b) per training iteration. For a < b < c, consistency matches a direct online transition a-to-c from a detached source to a stop-gradient EMA continuation b-to-c of the cached online prediction a-to-b. The detached source prevents this loss from updating the backbone; only the online predictor receives its gradients. The dashed EMA-update arrow denotes a moving-average update of predictor parameters, while the EMA output supplies the stopped-gradient target. Redundancy penalizes squared cosine similarity using one random pair (i, j) per training iteration, sampled independently of (a, b). sg denotes stop-gradient; EMA denotes the moving-average predictor.
