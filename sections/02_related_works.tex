\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Diffusion transformers}
Diffusion and flow-based models learn generative paths between data and noise~\citep{ho2020ddpm,song2021score,lipman2022flow}; surveys review their development in vision~\citep{croitoru2023p33}. DiT and SiT replace U-Net backbones with scalable transformers~\citep{peebles2023scalable,ma2024sit}, making depth-wise hidden representations a natural object of study. Recent analyses show that these intermediate representations are structured and that their quality is tied to generative performance~\citep{xiang2023denoising,mukhopadhyay2024,kim2025,stracke2025,an2025inductive}.

\paragraph{Representation learning with diffusion models}
Diffusion models can be viewed as multi-level denoising autoencoders~\citep{vincent2008extracting,song2019generative,ho2020ddpm}, and their intermediate features are discriminative enough for classification, segmentation, and other downstream tasks~\citep{xiang2023denoising,mukhopadhyay2024,kim2025,stracke2025}. Representation quality varies with depth, with the most semantic features typically found in intermediate layers rather than at the output~\citep{xiang2023denoising}. Related work trains a single model for both recognition and generation~\citep{yang2022hybrid,deja2023joint} or distills diffusion features into downstream backbones~\citep{yang2023repfusion,li2023dreamteacher}; a recent survey covers this connection in detail~\citep{fuest2026p12}. These methods read representations out of a diffusion model. LARA instead turns the relations between its layer representations into a training signal that improves generation.

\paragraph{Diffusion models with external representations}
REPA improves DiT and SiT training by aligning hidden states to external visual features~\citep{yu2024repa}. Follow-up methods extend this recipe through flexible guidance, end-to-end variants, structural or adversarial alignment, different teachers, spatially faithful targets, or entanglement with external representation tokens~\citep{wang2025flexible,wang2025haste,chen2025sara,leng2025repae,wang2026vaerepa,sun2026selftranscendence,singh2025irepa,wu2025reg}. These approaches use external encoders such as DINOv2~\citep{oquab2023dinov2}. Other methods use pretrained representations as an intermediate generation target, for example a compact semantic map or a self-supervised representation that conditions a second diffusion stage~\citep{pernias2024wurstchen,li2024rcg}. External representations accelerate training, but they require a suitable pretrained encoder for the target modality and add an encoder forward pass at every step~\citep{jiang2025sra,haghighi2026layersync}.

Recent work also examines how alignment is imposed. sREPA models spatial relational geometry beyond pointwise feature matching~\citep{xu2026srepa}, while MaskAlign applies alignment to sampled token subsets~\citep{pang2026maskalign}. These methods change the structure or token support of external alignment; LARA models transformations between internal representation spaces across depth. PixelREPA shows that direct external-feature regression can degrade pixel-space JiT training, motivating care when transferring alignment across representation spaces~\citep{shin2026pixelrepa}. A neighboring inference-time direction uses a representation alignment projector for training-free guidance during sampling~\citep{zu2026representationguidance}, whereas LARA supplies training-time supervision. Our experiments in \Cref{sec:external_alignment_compatibility} further show that LARA's layer-aware predictor is complementary to external alignment and can strengthen it.

\paragraph{Internal layer alignment and self-alignment}
Self-contained methods derive guidance from the diffusion model itself. One family builds self-supervised objectives into the diffusion transformer, such as masked modeling~\citep{zheng2023fast} or discriminative self-distillation from an EMA teacher~\citep{zhu2024sddit,tarvainen2017mean}. SRA likewise aligns earlier, noisier layers of the model with later layers of an EMA teacher~\citep{jiang2025sra}; these teacher-based methods require an additional forward pass per step. Dispersive and DiverseDiT regularize internal representation spread without an extra pass~\citep{wang2025dispersive,yang2026diversedit}, and LayerSync aligns selected weaker layers with stronger internal layers of the same model~\citep{haghighi2026layersync}. More broadly, redundancy-reduction objectives encourage decorrelated representations~\citep{zbontar2021barlow}. Recent diffusion-specific work extends this motivation to diversity across transformer blocks~\citep{li2026diverseditpp}. In LARA, redundancy control supports transition prediction by addressing the potential tension between broad cross-depth predictability and layer-specific structure. It does not seek maximal separation: in \Cref{fig:cosine_squared_heatmap}, the lowest measured cross-layer correlation does not coincide with the best generation quality. This setting is also related to feature distillation, where learned projectors transform student representations before matching a teacher~\citep{chen2026p05}. The relevant distinctions are target source (external teacher or internal states), relation (direct or structural matching or a learned transition), and supervision support (selected relations or multiple depths). LARA uses internal targets, learns an explicit mapping between representation spaces, and shares that mapping across multiple source--target depths.

\paragraph{Depth redundancy in deep networks}
Several works observe that deep networks contain partially redundant depth. In large language models, many deeper layers can be removed with limited loss~\citep{gromov2024unreasonable}, and diffusion models can reuse high-level features across denoising steps~\citep{ma2024deepcache} or adapt their depth and width to the timestep and spatial content~\citep{zhao2026p08}. These methods exploit redundancy to reduce inference cost. We instead skip blocks only as a diagnostic in \Cref{sec:inference_transition_diagnostic}, to test whether LARA's predicted states can stand in for the computation they replace.
