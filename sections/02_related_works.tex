\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Diffusion transformers}
Diffusion and flow-based models provide a continuous path between data and noise~\citep{ho2020ddpm,song2019score,song2021sde,song2020ddim,lipman2022flow}. DiT and SiT replace U-Net backbones with scalable transformers~\citep{ronneberger2015unet,vaswani2017attention,peebles2023scalable,ma2024sit}, making depth-wise hidden representations a natural object of study. Recent analyses show that these intermediate representations are structured and that their quality is tied to generative performance~\citep{xiang2023denoising,mukhopadhyay2024,kim2025,stracke2025,an2025inductive}.

\paragraph{Representation alignment for diffusion models}
REPA improves DiT and SiT training by aligning hidden states to external visual features~\citep{yu2024repa}. Follow-up methods extend this recipe through flexible guidance, end-to-end variants, structural or adversarial alignment, different teachers, or spatially faithful targets~\citep{wang2025flexible,wang2025haste,chen2025sara,leng2025repae,wang2026vaerepa,sun2026selftranscendence,singh2025irepa}. These approaches use external encoders such as DINOv2~\citep{oquab2023dinov2} and connect to predictive and denoising representation learning~\citep{vincent2008extracting,oord2018cpc}.

Recent work also examines how alignment is imposed. sREPA models spatial relational geometry beyond pointwise feature matching~\citep{xu2026srepa}, while MaskAlign applies alignment to sampled token subsets~\citep{pang2026maskalign}. These methods change the structure or token support of external alignment; LARA models transformations between internal representation spaces across depth. PixelREPA shows that direct external-feature regression can degrade pixel-space JiT training, motivating care when transferring alignment across representation spaces~\citep{shin2026pixelrepa}. A neighboring inference-time direction uses a representation alignment projector for training-free guidance during sampling~\citep{zu2026representationguidance}, whereas LARA supplies training-time supervision.

\paragraph{Internal layer alignment and self-alignment}
Self-contained methods derive guidance from the diffusion model itself. SRA uses an internal EMA teacher~\citep{jiang2025sra,tarvainen2017meanteacher}; Dispersive Loss and DiverseDiT regularize internal representation spread~\citep{wang2025dispersive,yang2026diversedit}; LayerSync aligns selected weaker layers with stronger internal layers~\citep{haghighi2026layersync}. More broadly, redundancy reduction has been studied through activation decorrelation, whitening, and covariance-based regularization~\citep{cogswell2016decov,huang2018decorrelated,huang2019iterative,zbontar2021barlow,bardes2022vicreg}. DiverseDiT++ further analyzes and promotes diversity across diffusion-transformer blocks~\citep{li2026diverseditpp}. In LARA, redundancy control supports transition prediction by addressing the potential tension between broad cross-depth predictability and layer-specific structure. It does not seek maximal separation: in \Cref{fig:cosine_squared_heatmap}, the lowest measured cross-layer correlation does not coincide with the best generation quality. This setting is also related to intermediate supervision and distillation, where hints, auxiliary heads, attention transfer, relational constraints, or self-distillation transfer information across representations~\citep{romero2015fitnets,lee2015deeply,zagoruyko2017attention,sun2019patient,park2019relational,furlanello2018born}. The relevant distinctions are target source (external teacher or internal states), relation (direct or structural matching or a learned transition), and supervision support (selected relations or multiple depths). LARA uses internal targets, learns an explicit mapping between representation spaces, and shares that mapping across multiple source--target depths.
