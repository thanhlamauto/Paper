\section{Experiment}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate our method on image generation and 3D human motion generation, and assess its transferability across different model architectures. Training settings, sampling protocols, and measurement definitions are reported in Supplementary Secs.~\ref{supp-app:experimental_details} and \ref{supp-app:metric_definitions}; design controls appear in \Cref{sec:ablation_study}.

\subsection{Image Generation}
\label{sec:image_generation}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

\noindent\textbf{Implementation details.}
Unless otherwise stated, we follow the class-conditional ImageNet training setup of SiT~\citep{ma2024sit}. We train on ImageNet~\citep{deng2009imagenet} at $256\times256$ resolution using the preprocessing protocol of ADM~\citep{dhariwal2021diffusion}. Each image is encoded by the Stable Diffusion VAE from LDM~\citep{rombach2022ldm} into a latent tensor $z\in\mathbb{R}^{32\times32\times4}$. We evaluate the \texttt{B/2}, \texttt{L/2}, and \texttt{XL/2} SiT configurations with patch size $2$. LARA trains the transition predictor jointly with the backbone using \Cref{eq:total_loss}. Unless specified, we use AdamW with learning rate $10^{-4}$ and batch size $256$. We sample source--target layer pairs uniformly from all valid pairs $(a,b)$ with $a<b$. Additional details on architecture, loss weights, cached-transition composition with an EMA target, training length, hardware, and compute budget are organized in Supplementary Sec.~\ref{supp-app:experimental_details}.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation.}
For each method in \Cref{tab:imagenet_no_cfg}, we report mean FID~\citep{heusel2017gans} over five sampling seeds (50K images per seed), following the SiT protocol~\citep{ma2024sit}. Inception Score (IS)~\citep{salimans2016improved}, precision, and recall~\citep{kynkaanniemi2019precision} are included in Supplementary \Cref{supp-tab:imagenet_cfg_full}; metric definitions and limitations are discussed in Supplementary Sec.~\ref{supp-app:metric_definitions}~\citep{stein2023exposing}.

\noindent\textbf{Comparison methods.}
Our controlled comparisons use the same SiT backbone and training budget as the base model, and compare against the self-contained internal baselines reported in \Cref{tab:imagenet_no_cfg}: Dispersive~\citep{wang2025dispersive}, SRA~\citep{jiang2025sra}, and LayerSync~\citep{haghighi2026layersync}.

In \Cref{tab:imagenet_cfg}, we compare latent diffusion transformers, including DiT, SiT, and REPA~\citep{peebles2023scalable,ma2024sit,yu2024repa}, alongside the internal baselines above under CFG, which affects the quality--diversity trade-off~\citep{kynkaanniemi2024guidance}. The full comparison with pixel diffusion, autoregressive, latent U-Net, and hybrid models is reported in Supplementary \Cref{supp-tab:imagenet_cfg_full}. Detailed baseline descriptions and the reporting protocol are in Supplementary Secs.~\ref{supp-app:baseline_descriptions} and \ref{supp-app:baseline_protocol}.

\noindent\textbf{Results.}
As shown in \Cref{tab:imagenet_no_cfg}, LARA consistently outperforms the SiT baseline and existing internal-alignment methods across model sizes and training budgets. On SiT-XL/2, it achieves $14\times$ faster convergence than standard SiT (\Cref{fig:training_curve}) and $2\times$ faster convergence than LayerSync, the strongest internal baseline, measured in training iterations: LARA at 1M iterations already surpasses LayerSync at 2M. This reduction more than offsets LARA's modest per-step overhead (\Cref{tab:internal_alignment_profile}). With CFG, LARA reaches 1.80 FID at 2M iterations, outperforming REPA and LayerSync in \Cref{tab:imagenet_cfg} without external representation guidance.

\subsection{Cross-Architecture Transfer}
\label{sec:cross_architecture}

LayerSync is the strongest internal-alignment baseline in our controlled SiT comparisons (\Cref{tab:imagenet_no_cfg}), achieving the lowest FID among the existing internal objectives at every reported backbone size and training budget. We therefore select it as the main alignment baseline for the architecture-transfer and motion-generation comparisons in \Cref{tab:cross_architecture,tab:humanml3d_results}.

To test whether transition learning transfers beyond SiT, we apply LARA to DiT-B and JiT-B on ImageNet $256\times256$. Both experiments use batch size 128, 300K training iterations, and AdamW at learning rate $10^{-4}$, with FID-10K as the evaluation metric.

\noindent\textbf{Results.}
As shown in \Cref{tab:cross_architecture}, LARA improves generation on both DiT-B and JiT-B, reducing FID-10K by 26.7\% and 30.3\%, respectively, over their vanilla counterparts under the same training budget. These gains across latent-space DiT and pixel-space JiT show that LARA's benefits extend beyond the SiT backbone.

\subsection{Text-Conditional Human Motion Generation}
\label{sec:human_motion_generation}

To test whether LARA transfers beyond image generation, we additionally evaluate text-conditioned human motion generation on HumanML3D using the MDM framework~\citep{guo2022generating,tevet2022mdm}. Given a text description, the model generates a sequence of human poses represented as 3D joint trajectories.

\noindent\textbf{Implementation details.}
We follow the MDM setup~\citep{tevet2022mdm} and use the same motion-transformer backbone for all rows. MDM and LayerSync are trained for $600\mathrm{K}$ iterations, while LARA is evaluated at the $300\mathrm{K}$ checkpoint reported in \Cref{tab:humanml3d_results}; the motion setup is described in Supplementary Sec.~\ref{supp-app:humanml3d_details}.

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation metrics.}
We report FID and R-Precision (top 3) using the standard HumanML3D evaluation protocol. Definitions and reporting conventions are provided in Supplementary Sec.~\ref{supp-app:metric_definitions}. Lower FID is better, while higher R-Precision indicates better text-motion retrieval alignment.

\subsection{Representation Analysis}
\label{sec:representation_analysis}

\noindent\textbf{Evaluation.}
We evaluate frozen \texttt{SiT-XL/2} features using linear probes for Tiny ImageNet classification and PASCAL VOC segmentation, and CKA~\citep{kornblith2019similarity} similarity to DINOv2-g.


\noindent\textbf{Results.}
As shown in \Cref{fig:representation_analysis}, LARA at $500\mathrm{K}$ iterations outperforms SiT at $2\mathrm{M}$ and LayerSync at $800\mathrm{K}$ on all three layer-averaged measures. Relative to LayerSync, classification accuracy, segmentation mIoU, and DINOv2-g CKA improve by $20.9\%$, $21.6\%$, and $9.5\%$, respectively. The gains extend across many intermediate layers, consistent with LARA's supervision over multiple layer pairs. These results demonstrate that LARA learns stronger semantic and spatial representations across depth with less training.

\section{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablation_study}

\textbf{Ablation overview.} We design the ablations around the main alternative explanations for LARA's gains. The objective could work only as generic regularization, depend on delicate loss weights, or require an oversized predictor. Each control changes one factor while keeping the backbone, training budget, and evaluation protocol fixed whenever possible. Further details of the matched controls appear in Supplementary Sec.~\ref{supp-app:realign_ablation_protocols}.

\subsection{Predictor Design and Capacity}
\label{sec:predictor_ablation}

\textbf{Predictor architecture.} Even a basic MLP predictor substantially improves generation over vanilla SiT and LayerSync, despite lacking explicit spatial inductive biases (\Cref{tab:predictor_architecture_ablation,tab:main_predictor_size_ablation}). Together with the motion-generation results in \Cref{sec:human_motion_generation}, this supports LARA's applicability across modalities. To maximize gains, the predictor should suit the modality: ConvNeXt + Attention performs best on images, consistent with the benefit of modeling spatial structure.

\subsection{Functional Layer-Aware Transition Diagnostic}
\label{sec:inference_transition_diagnostic}

\textbf{Functional transition diagnostic.} We further test whether the learned predictor produces target-depth states that are functionally compatible with the downstream backbone. \Cref{tab:transition_diagnostic} reports the results. For the transition from block 3 to block 7, we bypass the intervening transformer blocks and resume computation either directly from the unchanged block-3 state or from the predictor-generated state for block 7. This intervention isolates the contribution of the learned cross-depth transition while keeping the skipped interval fixed.

The difference is substantial. Naively bypassing the block-3-to-block-7 interval without the predictor degrades FID to 128.25. Replacing the same skipped computation with the predictor-generated target-depth state reduces FID to 54.17 while retaining the same relative block cost of $0.67\times$. Restricting the predictor transition to the first 125 of 250 denoising steps further improves FID to 45.84, approaching the full-backbone result of 35.12 while executing fewer transformer blocks overall.

The key comparison is therefore between bypassing the same depth interval with and without the learned transition. The reduction from 128.25 to 54.17 FID shows that the predictor supplies substantial information needed by the downstream layers, rather than merely benefiting from a different inference schedule. This provides functional evidence that LARA learns a target-depth representation that can be consumed by the remaining backbone. The standard generation results nevertheless use the full backbone; this intervention is intended as a diagnostic of the learned transition interface.
