Internal representation alignment can improve diffusion-transformer training without requiring an external representation teacher. Existing internal methods typically supervise selected cross-layer relations, although hidden-state transformations vary throughout depth. We observe that transition prediction difficulty varies substantially with both layer separation and source position. This motivates formulating internal alignment as learning a family of depth-conditioned cross-layer transitions. LARA uses a shared transition predictor conditioned on source depth, target depth, relative gap, timestep, and class. Compositional consistency encourages agreement between direct and composed transitions, while cross-layer redundancy control discourages excessive similarity between hidden states. Matched SiT-B/2 prediction-only controls isolate the two defining design choices: removing explicit depth conditioning worsens FID-50K from 39.48 to 42.40, while restricting the same predictor to the fixed pair $(4,7)$ gives 41.55, compared with 39.48 for multi-pair prediction. Adding consistency and redundancy regularization further reduces FID-50K to 34.43. These controls show empirical benefits in the tested setting, without implying superiority over every fixed pair. LARA improves controlled SiT results across the tested model scales and is additionally evaluated on DiT, JiT, and HumanML3D motion generation. These results support learning structured cross-depth transitions as an alternative to fixed-state internal matching.
