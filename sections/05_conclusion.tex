\section{Conclusion and Discussion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We study internal representation alignment as learning how representations transform across the depth of a diffusion transformer, rather than matching a single selected pair of hidden states. Our analysis shows that transition difficulty varies substantially with both layer separation and source position, motivating LARA's shared family of depth-conditioned cross-layer transitions. LARA combines multi-pair transition prediction with compositional consistency for difficult long-range mappings and cross-layer redundancy regularization to preserve layer-specific structure.

Across matched SiT training budgets, LARA consistently improves ImageNet generation over the tested internal-alignment baselines and maintains its advantage across B/2, L/2, and XL/2 model scales. The same transition-learning formulation also improves DiT and JiT backbones and transfers to text-conditioned HumanML3D generation, while layerwise probes show stronger representations across a broader portion of the evaluated hierarchy.

Controlled SiT-B/2 ablations further isolate the contribution of the transition formulation. Removing explicit depth conditioning degrades FID-50K from 39.48 to 42.40, while restricting the same depth-conditioned predictor to the fixed pair $(4,7)$ gives 41.55. Thus, the gain is not explained by adding a learned predictor alone: relation-specific conditioning and broader pair coverage both contribute to effective cross-depth supervision. Compositional consistency and redundancy regularization further improve the full objective to 34.43 FID-50K.

Together, these results establish cross-depth transition learning as a practical formulation for internal representation alignment, replacing fixed-pair matching with a shared, depth-aware model of how representations evolve through the diffusion-transformer hierarchy.

\paragraph{Limitations}
LARA builds on the premise that cross-layer transformations share enough reusable structure to be modeled by a common predictor, while depth conditioning captures relation-specific variation. Our representation and transition analyses support this premise in the tested diffusion transformers, revealing recurring structure across depth together with systematic changes in transition difficulty. Nevertheless, architectures with more abrupt changes in dimensionality, tokenization, or representation geometry may require corresponding adaptations to the transition interface. The compositional objective also relies on an EMA predictor to provide a stable target. Our ablation shows that the EMA target substantially improves over an online target, although a fuller characterization of how this stabilization interacts with different transition geometries remains an interesting direction.

LARA also introduces an explicit balance between cross-depth predictability and layer specialization. Multi-pair transition learning encourages information to remain recoverable across depth, whereas redundancy regularization prevents this shared structure from overwhelming layer-specific variation. The strength sweep shows that maximal decorrelation is not desirable: moderate redundancy reduction produces the best generation quality, while a stronger penalty can reduce cross-layer similarity without improving FID. This suggests that the optimal balance between reusable cross-depth structure and depth-specific specialization may depend on the representation hierarchy of the underlying backbone. Understanding this balance more systematically could further improve transition-based internal alignment.
