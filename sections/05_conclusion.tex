\section{Conclusion and Discussion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied internal representation alignment through the predictability of transitions between diffusion-transformer layers. Transition error varies with both layer separation and source position, indicating that one fixed cross-depth relation does not describe the full hierarchy. This observation motivated LARA's shared, depth-conditioned transition predictor. A compositional consistency loss encourages agreement between direct and two-step predictions, while a cross-layer redundancy term discourages excessive directional similarity.

Under matched SiT training budgets, LARA improves ImageNet generation FID over the internal-alignment baselines across the tested model sizes. The gains extend to the tested DiT and JiT backbones, and the motion experiment reports improved HumanML3D metrics at a shorter training budget. Layerwise probes show higher scores across more of the hierarchy for the evaluated LARA checkpoint, although those checkpoints were trained for different numbers of iterations. Component ablations identify transition prediction as a useful source of cross-depth supervision and show complementary effects from the consistency and redundancy terms; they do not isolate the contribution of depth conditioning or multi-pair sampling from a matched single-pair predictor. These results support learning cross-depth transitions as a way to use internal states for diffusion-transformer training.
