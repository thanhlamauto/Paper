\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Diffusion and flow models are widely used for high-fidelity image generation~\citep{ho2020ddpm,song2021sde,dhariwal2021diffusion,lipman2022flow}. Recent Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architectures combine diffusion modeling with transformer backbones and show the improvement as model size increases~\citep{peebles2023scalable,ma2024sit}. Despite these advances, training diffusion transformers remains computationally intensive, motivating growing interest in methods that improve representation learning to accelerate optimization and enhance generative performance~\citep{yu2024repa,jiang2025sra,haghighi2026layersync,wang2025dispersive}.

Representation alignment (RA) has been explored along two complementary directions. External methods transfer semantic structure from pretrained representation encoders such as DINOv2 or JEPA-style models~\citep{yu2024repa,oquab2023dinov2,chen2024jepa}. Internal methods derive supervision from representations within the diffusion transformer itself~\citep{jiang2025sra,haghighi2026layersync}. We focus on the internal setting, where source and target activations come from different network depths.

Internal alignment is challenging because activations at different depths carry different semantic information. Shallow layers primarily capture local patterns and low-level structure, whereas deeper layers encode more abstract concepts~\citep{mukhopadhyay2024,kim2025,stracke2025,raghu2021visiontransformers,gromov2024unreasonable}. Consequently, directly connecting these layers without manual tuning often yields suboptimal results, leading existing internal approaches to rely on aligning carefully selected layer pairs~\citep{jiang2025sra,haghighi2026layersync}. A fixed-pair objective supervises only one cross-depth relation, leaving relations between other layer pairs unused as alignment signals.
 
Pair-selection ablations in prior internal-alignment methods suggest that useful supervision extends beyond a single selected layer pair. LayerSync reports that multiple source--target choices improve over the unaligned SiT-XL/2 baseline, including $(6,18)$ and $(9,17)$~\citep{haghighi2026layersync}. SRA observes the same pattern on SiT-B/2, where several tested pairs outperform the baseline~\citep{jiang2025sra}. These pair-dependent gains suggest that useful cross-depth guidance is distributed across the hierarchy. This motivates moving beyond fixed-pair matching toward learning a shared family of cross-depth representation transitions.

To learn these transitions jointly, we introduce \emph{LARA}, a multi-pair alignment framework built around a shared, layer-aware transition predictor and two complementary objectives. Directly aligning hidden states across many depths is difficult because their statistics and semantic content vary throughout the hierarchy. LARA addresses this mismatch by predicting each target-layer representation from a source state, with the mapping conditioned on the source and target depths. Extending prediction across many layer pairs introduces two further challenges. First, broad cross-depth predictability can make hidden states overly similar, so a cross-layer redundancy loss encourages layer-specific structure. Second, long-range transitions are harder to learn directly; decomposing them into shorter transitions provides an easier intermediate learning path. We therefore introduce a compositional consistency loss that encourages a direct long-range prediction to agree with the composition of shorter transitions.

Our study makes three contributions. First, we formulate internal representation alignment as learning a shared family of depth-conditioned transitions instead of matching one fixed pair of hidden states. Second, we introduce LARA's layer-conditioned predictor, compositional consistency objective, and cross-layer redundancy regularizer as a joint training framework. Third, controlled experiments clarify the role of each part of our layer-aware framework, including explicit depth conditioning and multi-pair supervision. We further show that LARA generalizes across latent-space and pixel-space image generation, as well as text-conditioned motion generation, using SiT at multiple scales, DiT, and JiT.
