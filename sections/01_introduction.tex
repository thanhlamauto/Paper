\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Diffusion and flow models have become an effective paradigm for high-fidelity image generation~\citep{ho2020ddpm,song2021sde,dhariwal2021diffusion,lipman2022flow}. Recent Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architectures further improve scalability and generative quality by combining diffusion modeling with the strong sequence modeling capability of transformers~\citep{peebles2023scalable,ma2024sit}. Despite these advances, training diffusion transformers remains computationally intensive, motivating growing interest in methods that improve representation learning to accelerate optimization and enhance generative performance~\citep{yu2024repa,jiang2025sra,haghighi2026layersync,wang2025dispersive}.

Representation alignment (RA) has been explored along two complementary directions. External methods transfer semantic structure from pretrained representation encoders such as DINOv2 or JEPA-style models~\citep{yu2024repa,oquab2023dinov2,chen2024jepa}. Internal methods derive supervision from representations within the diffusion transformer itself~\citep{jiang2025sra,haghighi2026layersync}. We focus on the internal setting, where source and target activations come from different network depths.

Internal alignment is challenging because activations at different depths carry different semantic information. Shallow layers primarily capture local patterns and low-level structure, whereas deeper layers encode more abstract concepts~\citep{mukhopadhyay2024,kim2025,stracke2025,raghu2021visiontransformers,gromov2024unreasonable}. Directly matching these activations can therefore be difficult. Existing internal approaches often align a selected layer pair~\citep{jiang2025sra,haghighi2026layersync}. A predictor designed for one source--target relation does not explicitly model how that relation changes across other depths.

Even a well-chosen fixed pair provides supervision for only one source--target relation in a hierarchy whose representations vary with depth. The transformation between hidden states depends on both source and target depth, so treating internal alignment as a single matching problem leaves other cross-layer relations unmodeled. Internal representation alignment is therefore not merely a problem of finding which layers to align, but of modeling how representations transform throughout the network hierarchy. This motivates learning a family of depth-dependent transitions.

Following this perspective, we propose \emph{LARA}, which learns a shared transition predictor across source--target layer pairs. Rather than forcing hidden states at different depths to match directly, LARA predicts a target-layer representation in its own coordinate system. Shared parameters capture reusable cross-depth structure, while source-layer, target-layer, and timestep conditioning adapt the mapping to each relation. Learning multiple transitions also raises two challenges: direct predictions should agree with compositions of shorter transitions, and broad cross-depth predictability can make layer representations overly similar. LARA addresses the first with a transition-consistency objective and a slowly updated target predictor, and the second with a diversity regularizer that discourages layer homogenization.

Our study makes three contributions. First, we formulate internal representation alignment as learning a shared family of depth-conditioned transitions instead of matching one fixed pair of hidden states. Second, we introduce LARA's layer-conditioned predictor, compositional consistency objective, and diversity regularizer as a joint training framework. Third, we evaluate the framework across SiT backbones and text-conditioned motion generation, and examine representation quality, predictor capacity, and training cost.
