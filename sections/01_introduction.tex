\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Diffusion and flow models are widely used for high-fidelity image generation~\citep{ho2020ddpm,song2021sde,dhariwal2021diffusion,lipman2022flow}. Recent Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architectures combine diffusion modeling with transformer backbones and show gains as model size increases~\citep{peebles2023scalable,ma2024sit}. Despite these advances, training diffusion transformers remains computationally intensive, motivating growing interest in methods that improve representation learning to accelerate optimization and enhance generative performance~\citep{yu2024repa,jiang2025sra,haghighi2026layersync,wang2025dispersive}.

Representation alignment (RA) has been explored along two complementary directions. External methods transfer semantic structure from pretrained representation encoders such as DINOv2 or JEPA-style models~\citep{yu2024repa,oquab2023dinov2,chen2024jepa}. Internal methods derive supervision from representations within the diffusion transformer itself~\citep{jiang2025sra,haghighi2026layersync}. We focus on the internal setting, where source and target activations come from different network depths.

Internal alignment is challenging because activations at different depths carry different semantic information. Shallow layers primarily capture local patterns and low-level structure, whereas deeper layers encode more abstract concepts~\citep{mukhopadhyay2024,kim2025,stracke2025,raghu2021visiontransformers,gromov2024unreasonable}. Directly matching these activations can therefore be difficult. Existing internal approaches often align a selected layer pair~\citep{jiang2025sra,haghighi2026layersync}. A fixed-pair objective supervises only one cross-depth relation and does not explicitly model how that relation varies elsewhere in the hierarchy.

Our transition diagnostic shows that prediction error varies with both layer separation and source position (\Cref{fig:projection_layout_observation}(b,c)). A single matching objective leaves these other cross-layer relations unsupervised. Internal representation alignment thus raises a question beyond which layers to align: how do their representations transform throughout the network hierarchy? This motivates learning a family of depth-dependent transitions.

Following this perspective, we propose \emph{LARA}, which learns a shared transition predictor across source--target layer pairs. Rather than forcing hidden states at different depths to match directly, LARA predicts a target-layer representation in its own coordinate system. Shared parameters capture reusable cross-depth structure, while explicit source-layer, target-layer, and gap conditioning make the mapping relation-specific. Multi-pair supervision broadens coverage across the hierarchy, but also exposes the predictor to relations with heterogeneous difficulty, especially at larger layer gaps. Moreover, encouraging many layers to be mutually predictable may make their hidden states overly similar. These considerations motivate two complementary objectives: compositional consistency encourages direct transitions to agree with compositions of shorter ones, using a slowly updated target predictor, while cross-layer redundancy reduction discourages excessive directional similarity to preserve layer-specific structure.

Our study makes three contributions. First, we formulate internal representation alignment as learning a shared family of depth-conditioned transitions instead of matching one fixed pair of hidden states. Second, we introduce LARA's layer-conditioned predictor, compositional consistency objective, and cross-layer redundancy regularizer as a joint training framework. Third, we evaluate the framework across multiple diffusion-transformer backbones, including SiT, DiT, and JiT, and further test its transfer to text-conditioned motion generation, together with analyses of representation quality, predictor capacity, and training cost.
